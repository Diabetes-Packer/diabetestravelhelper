Flying with Freestyle Libre: Airport Security Guide (2026)

The Freestyle Libre 2 and Libre 3 are among the most widely used CGMs in the world. Abbott's airport guidance comes down to one rule that matters more than everything else combined: keep the sensor away from full-body scanners. Here is the full picture, in plain language.

Updated September 2026  ·  7 min read  ·  By Diabetes Travel Helper

I don't wear a Freestyle Libre. I'm a Dexcom user, so this isn't a personal travel diary. It is the next most useful thing: Abbott's published airport guidance, translated into plain language, plus the TSA procedure that applies no matter which CGM is on your arm.

Short version for the impatient: walk through the metal detector like normal. If they point you at a full-body scanner, ask for a pat-down instead. That is 90 percent of it.

What Abbott Says About the Libre at Security

Abbott's guidance is direct. Here it is without the legal padding:

  • Walk-through metal detectors: fine. Abbott states that common electrostatic and electromagnetic interference, including conventional airport metal detectors, cause no problems for the sensor.
  • Full-body scanners that use X-ray or millimeter radio-wave: do not expose your sensor. Abbott says the effect of these scanners has not been evaluated, and exposure may damage the sensor or cause inaccurate readings.
  • If you go through a full-body scanner, Abbott says you must remove your sensor. So don't go through one. Request another type of screening instead.
  • Abbott publishes a printable Airport Security Certificate. It explains to officers that your sensor can't come off without ending the session, and it asks for alternate screening. Save it to your phone before you fly.
  • The Libre reader is classified as a medical portable electronic device and meets the emissions standards for safe use on aircraft.
Read the scanner rule literally. Abbott doesn't say the scanner is "probably fine." They say the effect hasn't been evaluated, and that going through one means removing your sensor. A pat-down takes five minutes. A dead sensor on day one costs you up to two weeks of readings. Easy trade.

What to Say at Security

"I'm wearing a Freestyle Libre glucose sensor. It's a medical device, and I can't remove it without destroying it. I'd like alternative screening, please." If the officer hesitates, show Abbott's Airport Security Certificate on your phone. It does the explaining for you. You can download it from Abbott's support page.

Libre 2 vs. Libre 3: Any Difference?

No. Abbott's airport guidance is identical across the Libre 2, 2 Plus, 3, and 3 Plus. The practical difference is how you check readings. The Libre 2 needs a scan with your reader or phone. The Libre 3 streams to your phone automatically. At the checkpoint your phone sits in a tray for a few minutes either way, so expect a short gap in readings. The sensor keeps recording the whole time. Nothing to worry about.

Using the Libre on the Plane

Airplane mode on, Bluetooth back on. Same drill as every CGM. One Libre-specific detail from the user manual: alarms will not sound while your phone is in airplane mode unless Bluetooth is on. If you depend on low-glucose alarms, especially on overnight flights, don't skip that step. The reader itself works normally for the whole flight.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I wear my Freestyle Libre through the airport body scanner?

No. Abbott says not to expose the sensor to full-body scanners that use X-ray or millimeter radio-wave. The effect has not been evaluated and may damage the sensor or cause inaccurate readings. Request alternate screening. Abbott states that if you go through one, you must remove your sensor.

Is the walk-through metal detector safe for the Freestyle Libre?

Yes. Abbott states that common electrostatic and electromagnetic interference, including conventional airport metal detectors, cause no problems for the sensor.

Can spare Libre sensors go through the baggage X-ray?

Yes. Abbott's scanner warning applies to the sensor you are wearing. Sealed spare sensors in your bag have no published restriction and go through the X-ray belt with your other belongings.

Is there a difference between Libre 2 and Libre 3 at airport security?

No. Abbott's airport guidance is identical across the Libre 2, Libre 2 Plus, Libre 3, and Libre 3 Plus.

Can I use my Freestyle Libre on the plane?

Yes. Put your phone in airplane mode and turn Bluetooth back on manually. Note that alarms will not sound in airplane mode unless Bluetooth is enabled, so confirm this if you rely on low-glucose alarms.

How many Libre sensors should I pack for a trip?

Libre 2 and Libre 3 sensors each last 14 days. Bring two sensors for trips up to two weeks and three for three to four weeks. Always pack one spare beyond what the math requires.
