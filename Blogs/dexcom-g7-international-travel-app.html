Dexcom G7 International Travel: App Region Locks and How to Prepare (2026)

The G7 phone app is region-locked at install, and that one quirk ruins more international trips than any other. Here is how it works, the one mistake to avoid, and the backup that makes it a non-issue.

Updated September 2026  ·  6 min read

Picture this. You land in another country, something glitches with your G7 app, and you do the obvious thing: delete it and reinstall. Except the reinstall fails. The app will not install outside your home country, and now you have no CGM display for the rest of the trip.

This is not a hypothetical. Dexcom's own G7 user guide states the app must be installed in your country of residence, and traveler forums are full of people who learned this the hard way. I am still on the G6 myself, and the G6 never did this, which is exactly why so many upgraders get blindsided.

How the Region Lock Actually Works

When you install the Dexcom G7 app for the first time, or when a G6 user installs it as an upgrade, the app checks that the country you are in matches the country of residence on your Dexcom account. Dexcom says this is to make sure you get the features and resources approved for where you live. Whatever the reason, the effect is simple: first-time installs happen at home or not at all.

Here is the part people miss. The check happens at install time. An app that is already installed and working keeps working abroad. Travelers report normal operation on international trips as long as nobody deletes the app mid-trip. So the lock is not a travel ban. It is a reinstall trap.

Do not uninstall the G7 app while you are abroad. If something looks wrong, sign out and sign back in. Do not delete it. A deleted app outside your home country may refuse to reinstall until you are back on home soil, and that is a long trip without CGM data.

The Receiver Is Your Insurance Policy

Dexcom's own international travel guidance says it plainly: if you have a receiver, it makes a great backup to your phone app. Bring it.

The receiver has no app store, no region check, and no install step. It pairs with your sensor and shows readings anywhere on earth. Phone lost, stolen, drowned in a hotel sink, or simply refusing to reinstall the app: the receiver does not care. It just works.

Pack it with its USB cable and charger, plus a plug adapter for your destination. If you do not already own a receiver, ask Dexcom about getting one before a big international trip. It is the cheapest trip insurance a G7 user can buy.

Before You Leave: The Ten-Minute Prep

Do all of this at home, on your own wifi, with no time pressure:

  • Install and fully set up the G7 app before departure. Never plan a first install or a G6-to-G7 upgrade for the week of an international trip.
  • Update the app to the latest version while you are still home. Do not let the first update happen on hotel wifi in another country.
  • I turn off auto-updates on my phone before international trips, so nothing about my diabetes setup changes mid-trip without my say-so.
  • Pack the receiver even if you never touch it at home. It earns its place in the bag the one time your phone does not cooperate.
  • Save Dexcom's support contact for your home country somewhere you can find it offline. If something breaks abroad, their guide says to contact support in your country of residence and tell them you are traveling internationally.

If Your Phone Dies Abroad

This is the scenario the prep above exists for. Phone gone, app gone with it, and you are standing in a foreign city.

Step one: switch to the receiver. That is the entire plan. No scrambling, no app store, no location errors.

Step two is knowing what will not help. Buying a replacement phone abroad does not fix the app problem, because the install check is about where you are, not which phone you hold. Do not waste a day phone shopping for a software issue.

Step three: your fingerstick meter is the final fallback. Keep it in your day bag, not buried in your hotel room, so it is there during the gap between losing the phone and getting the receiver running.

Sensors and Supplies Abroad

Bring more sensors than the trip math says you need. Buying replacements abroad means navigating foreign pharmacies, local prescriptions, and regional compatibility questions you do not want to solve mid-vacation. Pack the spares at home and do not plan to shop.

Everything diabetes-related rides in your carry-on. That rule never changes, and international trips are the worst possible time to learn why.

A Note on Time Zones

The G7 handles time zone changes on its own and your readings stay continuous across zones. Insulin timing across zones is a separate problem with its own tool. If your trip crosses several time zones, run your itinerary through the Time Zone Planner before you fly.

Frequently Asked Questions

Can I install the Dexcom G7 app while traveling abroad?

No. Dexcom's user guide states the G7 app must be installed in your country of residence. On first install, the app verifies that your location matches the country on your Dexcom account. Plan any first-time install or G6-to-G7 upgrade for before you leave home.

Does the G7 app keep working internationally if it was already installed?

Yes. The location verification happens at install time, and travelers report the app works normally abroad once it is set up. The problem only arises if you need to install or reinstall the app while outside your home country.

What should I do if I need to reinstall the G7 app mid-trip?

Do not uninstall it. Sign out and sign back in instead. If the app is already deleted and will not reinstall abroad, use your Dexcom receiver for the rest of the trip and contact Dexcom technical support in your country of residence.

Should I bring the Dexcom receiver on international trips?

Yes. Dexcom itself recommends the receiver as a backup for international travel. It works anywhere with no app involved, so a lost phone or an app problem abroad does not leave you without readings.

Did the Dexcom G6 have this region lock problem?

No. Travelers report the G6 app had no location verification at install, so it could be reinstalled anywhere. The G7 added the country check, which is why people upgrading from the G6 get caught out.

What if my phone is lost or stolen abroad?

Use your Dexcom receiver. That is exactly the scenario it exists for. A replacement phone bought abroad will not solve the app problem, because the install check is about your location, not the phone. Keep a fingerstick meter as the final fallback.
