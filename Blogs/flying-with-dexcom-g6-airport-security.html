Flying with Dexcom G6: Airport Security Guide (2026)

The Dexcom G6 is the one CGM where the airport body scanner question actually matters. Dexcom's guidance for the G6 is stricter than for newer sensors, and getting it wrong can cost you a sensor mid-trip. Here is exactly what to do.

Updated September 2026  ·  8 min read  ·  By Diabetes Travel Helper

I fly with a Dexcom G6. Have for years. The most boring thing I can tell you about it: nothing has ever happened at airport security. No alarms that mattered. No damaged sensors. No memorable incidents at all. That is not luck. It is what happens when you know the rules for this specific device.

The G6 has one rule that surprises people, especially anyone who has read about the G7. Dexcom specifically advises G6 users to stay out of the AIT body scanners, the ones where you stand with your arms raised. Everything else is straightforward. Below is Dexcom's official guidance, what I actually do at the checkpoint, and how to pack.

What Dexcom Officially Says About the G6 at Security

Dexcom publishes a dedicated airport security page for the G6. In plain terms, here is what it says:

  • Walk-through metal detectors: safe. Wear your G6 straight through.
  • Hand-wanding: safe.
  • Full-body pat-down with visual inspection: safe. This is what Dexcom recommends if you want to play it safe.
  • AIT body scanners (millimeter wave): Dexcom recommends avoiding them. Their reasoning is honest. They have not tested every scanner and X-ray machine in existence, so they cannot guarantee your G6 will be unaffected.
  • Baggage X-ray: do not put G6 components through it. Place spare sensors, transmitters, and applicators in a separate bag and hand it to the Security Officer.
One detail from Dexcom's user guide: if you wear the G6 through a walk-through metal detector, use your finger-stick meter for treatment decisions until you are past the security area. Not the CGM. Just until you are through.
G6 vs. G7: the critical difference. Dexcom has cleared the G7 for AIT body scanners. The G6 does not have that clearance. If you recently switched from G6 to G7, or you are about to, your airport routine changes with the device. Do not assume the rules carry over.

What to Say at Security

Before you reach the scanner, tell the officer: "I'm wearing a Dexcom G6 continuous glucose monitor. It is a medical device under my skin and I can't remove it. I'd like a pat-down, please." That is the whole script. Short, specific. It tells them what the device is, why it can't come off, and what you want instead.

What I Actually Do (The Honest Version)

Here is the honest part. I don't request the pat-down. I didn't know opting out was a thing until a few years ago. Since then I have just walked through the body scanner with my G6 on, like everyone else. No sensor has failed afterward. No strange readings I could trace back to security. No issues of any kind.

That is my experience. It is not a recommendation. Dexcom's official guidance says to avoid the AIT scanners with a G6, and the official guidance is the safe play. If a dead sensor on day two of a trip would ruin the trip for you, take the pat-down. It costs a few extra minutes, and it is the option Dexcom stands behind.

Why do I still use the scanner? Convenience, plus a few dozen uneventful trips behind me. Would I do it with a brand-new sensor on the morning of a two-week international trip? That day, I would probably take the pat-down. Risk tolerance is personal. Just make it an informed choice instead of a habit you never questioned.

Using the G6 on the Plane

Past security, the G6 works normally for the entire flight. The setup takes ten seconds:

  1. Put your phone in airplane mode.
  2. Turn Bluetooth back on manually. Airplane mode switches it off.
  3. Leave the Dexcom app running. Readings keep arriving over Bluetooth.
  4. Using the receiver instead of a phone? Just leave it on. It doesn't need airplane mode.

Keep your phone or receiver where you can reach it. That is the whole in-flight routine.

How Many G6 Sensors to Pack

Each G6 sensor lasts 10 days. The transmitter lasts about 90 days, so if yours is nearing the end of that window, pack a spare transmitter too.

The rule never changes: pack one more sensor than the math says you need. Sensors get knocked off. They fail early. They end up in hotel sinks. The spare weighs nothing and saves trips.

Keeping the Sensor Stuck During Travel

Heat, sweat, swimming, and long active days loosen CGM adhesive faster than normal life. Two things handle nearly all of it. First, wipe an adhesive barrier onto clean, dry skin before you place the sensor. Second, press overpatch tape over the sensor edges for beach days, hikes, or anything sweaty. One more habit: change your sensor a day or two before departure, not the morning of. A sensor with 48 hours to settle holds better and reads more accurately.

G6 Travel Checklist

  • ☐ Pack one more sensor than your trip math requires
  • ☐ Pack a spare transmitter if yours is near 90 days
  • ☐ Pack overpatch tape and adhesive barrier wipes
  • ☐ Pack your backup meter and test strips. Keep the meter in your pocket through security, not in the X-ray bag
  • ☐ Change your sensor 1 to 2 days before departure
  • ☐ Decide your security plan before you get in line: pat-down request or metal detector
  • ☐ Spare sensors go in a separate bag for hand inspection, never on the X-ray belt
  • ☐ On the plane: airplane mode on, Bluetooth on, app running
  • ☐ Everything in your carry-on. Never in checked luggage

Frequently Asked Questions

Can the Dexcom G6 go through airport body scanners?

Dexcom advises G6 users to avoid AIT body scanners (millimeter wave). Their position is that they have not tested every scanner in existence, so they cannot guarantee the G6 will be unaffected. Request a pat-down or walk through the metal detector instead.

Can I wear my G6 through the walk-through metal detector?

Yes. Dexcom lists walk-through metal detectors as safe for the G6. One caveat from the user guide: use your finger-stick meter, not the CGM, for treatment decisions until you are past the security area.

Do spare G6 sensors go through the baggage X-ray?

No. Dexcom says not to put G6 components through X-ray machines. Place spare sensors, transmitters, and applicators in a separate bag and hand it to the Security Officer for hand inspection.

What is the difference between the G6 and G7 at airport security?

The Dexcom G7 is cleared by Dexcom for AIT body scanners. The G6 is not. If you have switched from G6 to G7, your airport routine changes with the device.

Can I use the Dexcom G6 on the plane?

Yes. Put your phone in airplane mode, turn Bluetooth back on manually, and leave the Dexcom app running. Readings continue normally. If you use the receiver, just leave it on.

How many G6 sensors should I pack for a trip?

G6 sensors last 10 days each. Pack one more than your trip requires: 2 sensors for a week, 3 for two weeks. If your transmitter is near the end of its roughly 90-day life, bring a spare.
