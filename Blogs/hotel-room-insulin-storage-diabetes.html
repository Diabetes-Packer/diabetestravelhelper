Hotel Rooms with Diabetes: Insulin Storage Rules for Travel (2026)

At home, insulin storage is a habit: the fridge does the work. On a trip, every hotel night is a small experiment: the minibar runs warm, the room heater runs hot, and the spare pens sit somewhere you cannot control. Holiday travel stacks the risk, because it stacks the nights.

The good news is that the rules are simpler than the worry. Insulin divides into two buckets, and the manufacturers' labels agree on both. Here are the rules, the hotel traps, and the checklist.

General guidance only, not medical advice. Storage windows differ by insulin brand and product, so the label on your own pen or vial wins every argument below. Confirm your plan with your care team before you travel.

The three rules that cover everything

  • Unopened insulin stays refrigerated. The standard is 36 to 46F (2 to 8C), until the expiration date printed on the label. This is the rule NovoLog, Humalog, and Lantus labels all state.
  • Opened insulin lives at room temperature. Once a pen or vial is in use, it is good at room temperature below 86F (30C) for 28 days from first use, even if insulin is left inside. All three major labels say the same thing: do not put an in-use pen back in the fridge.
  • Never freeze insulin. Freezing destroys insulin's effectiveness, and the damage does not reverse when it thaws. If insulin has been frozen, the label answer is the same everywhere: do not use it.

The two buckets: the pen you are using vs the spares

This split is the whole system, and it is what the labels are written around.

  • The pen or vial you are using. Keep it at room temperature, cap on, out of direct sun and heat. The labels for NovoLog FlexPen and FlexTouch, Humalog KwikPen, and Lantus SoloSTAR all say the same: do not refrigerate an in-use pen. There is a comfort reason too: the ADA notes that insulin in use may be kept at room temperature partly because cold insulin can sting at the injection site.
  • The spares. Unopened pens, vials, and cartridges go in the fridge at 36 to 46F until you need them. And here is the part travelers underuse: even unopened NovoLog, Humalog, and Lantus are good at room temperature below 86F for 28 days. A one-week trip does not require a fridge for the spares at all. The labels say so; most travelers just do not believe them at first.

Pump users get a special case: the insulin in a pump reservoir is always warm, because it is on your body. NovoLog's label says to change the insulin in the reservoir at least every 6 days, or sooner after exposure to temperatures above 98.6F (37C). Your pump's own manual sets the site schedule, usually shorter; the reservoir rule is about heat, not the site.

The hotel fridge problem

The room fridge is the trap. Minibar fridges are built to chill drinks, not to hold a steady 36 to 46F: they run warm, they run erratic, and some have freezer compartments or freeze spots along the back wall. Insulin stored against a freeze spot can freeze without you noticing, and frozen insulin is ruined insulin.

  • Ask for a medical fridge, not a minibar. Many hotels will provide a small fridge or store medication in the staff fridge free of charge when you explain it is insulin. Ask at check-in, before you unpack.
  • Never use the freezer compartment. Not for a quick chill, not overnight. Frozen means discarded, per every label.
  • Keep spares away from the back wall. If the room fridge is your only option, keep insulin toward the middle, where freeze spots are least likely.
  • No fridge at all is fine for most trips. If your trip is shorter than your label's room-temperature window (28 days below 86F for NovoLog, Humalog, and Lantus), the spares can ride at room temperature the whole time. Keep them out of heat and sun, the same as your in-use pen.

Hot rooms and cars: the other temperature enemy

Cold gets the attention, but heat is the more common travel killer. Insulin kept above 86F loses potency over time, and a parked car or a sun-facing windowsill can get there in an hour. Never leave insulin in a parked car, and never stash the day bag in direct sun. For hot-climate trips, the evaporative cooling wallets covered in the keep-insulin-cool guide hold insulin in range without a fridge and without ice.

How to tell if your insulin went bad

Insulin that has been cooked or frozen does not always look different, so inspection is a habit, not a rescue plan:

  • Look before every injection. The ADA's standing advice: inspect the insulin for clumping, frosting, precipitation, or any change in clarity or color. Rapid-acting insulins like NovoLog and Humalog, and glargine like Lantus, should be clear; cloudy insulins should be uniformly cloudy.
  • Suspect potency before you blame your body. The ADA also notes that unexplained high glucose can be a sign of lost potency. If your numbers run high for no reason after a heat or freeze scare, open a fresh pen or vial and talk to your care team.
  • When in doubt, replace it. If you are not sure about a pen's history, the official guidance is blunt: swap it for one you trust. On a trip, that is exactly what the 20% extra supply buffer is for.

The hotel insulin checklist

  1. Split the buckets: in-use pen at room temperature in the day bag, spares in the room.
  2. Spares go in a real fridge at 36 to 46F, never the minibar freezer compartment, never against a freeze spot.
  3. No fridge? Room temperature below 86F is fine for trips under your label's 28-day window.
  4. Write the first-use date on every pen so the 28-day clock is visible.
  5. Keep sugar packets at the bedside, 1 at 60 to 70, 2 below 60, same as home.
  6. Pack 20% more insulin than the trip math says, split so one lost bag is never all of it.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does insulin have to stay in the hotel fridge?

Only the spares. The pen or vial you are currently using stays at room temperature: NovoLog, Humalog, and Lantus labels all say not to refrigerate in-use pens. Unopened insulin is stored refrigerated at 36 to 46F (2 to 8C), but even unopened insulin is good at room temperature below 86F (30C) for 28 days per the manufacturer labels.

Can I store insulin in the hotel minibar?

It is risky. Minibar fridges run warm or erratic, and some have freezer spots that can freeze insulin, which ruins it. A safer move is to ask the front desk for a medical fridge or for storage in the staff fridge. Never put insulin in a freezer compartment.

How long can spare insulin go without a fridge on a trip?

Up to 28 days at room temperature below 86F (30C) for NovoLog, Humalog, and Lantus, per their FDA labels. Keep it out of heat and direct sun, and always check your own insulin's label, since the window differs by product.

What should I do if my insulin froze in the hotel fridge?

Do not use it. Every major insulin label says to discard insulin that has been frozen, because freezing destroys its effectiveness even after it thaws. Open a fresh pen or vial from your spare supply.

Can I put my in-use pen back in the fridge at night?

No. NovoLog, Humalog, and Lantus pen labels all say not to refrigerate an in-use pen. Keep the pen you are using at room temperature, cap on, away from heat and light, and discard it 28 days after first use.
