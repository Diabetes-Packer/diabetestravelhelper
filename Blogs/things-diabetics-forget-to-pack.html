4. Adhesive wipes for heat, humidity, and swimming

Sensors and pods stick fine at home, then start peeling on day two of a beach vacation. Heat, sweat, sunscreen, and pool water are the enemy of adhesives. A few adhesive barrier wipes in your kit keep everything stuck down when the climate works against you.

5. Paper copies of your prescriptions

If you lose supplies or need an emergency refill abroad, a paper prescription helps a foreign pharmacy help you. Include the generic drug names, not just brand names, because insulin brands differ by country. Keep a copy in your bag and a photo on your phone.

6. A medical ID

If a severe low leaves you unable to communicate, a medical ID tells responders what is happening in seconds. It is one of the most recommended travel items for diabetics and one of the most commonly skipped.

7. Hand sanitizer or wet wipes

Accurate fingerstick readings need clean, dry hands, and there is not always a sink around when you need to test. Residue from food or fruit on your fingers can throw off a reading. A small bottle of sanitizer or a pack of wipes solves it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What do most diabetics forget to pack for travel?

The most commonly forgotten items are the supporting ones, not insulin itself: a portable charger for the phone running your CGM app, adhesive wipes to keep sensors on in heat and humidity, a crushproof case for fragile gear, and paper copies of prescriptions.

Why should I bring a portable charger when traveling with diabetes?

If your CGM readings or pump controls run through your phone, a dead phone means no glucose data and no remote bolusing. A small power bank keeps your diabetes tech running through long travel days, delays, and power outages.

Do I need a crushproof case for diabetes supplies?

You do not strictly need one, but baggage handlers, car trunks, and adventure travel are rough on meters, vials, and pumps. A small waterproof crushproof case keeps fragile gear organized and protected for little weight.

Should I carry paper copies of my prescriptions when traveling with diabetes?

Yes. If you lose supplies or need an emergency refill abroad, a paper prescription with generic drug names helps a foreign pharmacy help you. Insulin brand names differ by country, so generic names matter.
