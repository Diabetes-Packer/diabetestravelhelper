Traveling with GLP-1 Medications: Ozempic, Wegovy, Mounjaro, and Zepbound Travel Rules (2026)

GLP-1 medications are the easiest diabetes drugs to travel with, and it is not close. They are dosed once a week, and every label gives you a generous room-temperature window, so most trips need no cooler at all. There is exactly one hard rule, and it is the same on all four labels: never freeze the pen.

Updated October 2026  ·  7 min read

GLP-1 stands for glucagon-like peptide-1. These receptor agonist medications, Ozempic and Wegovy (semaglutide), Mounjaro and Zepbound (tirzepatide), are injected once a week, which already makes them simpler travel companions than anything dosed daily. The labels then do you another favor: each one spells out exactly how long the pen survives outside the fridge. Learn your number and most of the travel anxiety disappears. As always, this is general guidance, not medical advice. Your care team owns your medication plan.

A note for the type 1 readers of this site: Ozempic's label states it is not a substitute for insulin and is not for use in people with type 1 diabetes. This guide is for the type 2 readers, and the travel companions, who do use these medications.

The storage numbers, per the labels

Every GLP-1 pen lives in the refrigerator at 36F to 46F (2C to 8C) in its original carton, protected from light. The room-temperature allowances differ, and this is the number that decides whether your trip needs a cooler:

Ozempic. The multi-dose pen keeps for 56 days at room temperature (59F to 86F) after first use, or it can stay refrigerated. Always remove the needle after each injection and store the pen without a needle attached, using a new needle every time.

Wegovy. The single-dose pen can be kept at 46F to 86F for up to 28 days before the cap comes off. The needle is built in and hidden, so there is nothing to attach or prime. The pen is discarded after the single dose.

Mounjaro. Single-dose pens and vials can be kept at room temperature up to 86F for up to 21 days total, then must be discarded. The multi-dose vial and KwikPen get 30 days at room temperature, or 30 days after first use, or four weekly doses, whichever comes first.

Zepbound. Single-dose pens and vials can be kept at room temperature up to 86F for up to 21 days. Once a pen has been out at room temperature, do not put it back in the fridge.

The practical read: for any trip shorter than your medication's window, the pen rides in your bag at room temperature and you do not think about cooling at all. A two-week holiday needs no cooler for any of these. The cooler only enters the picture for unopened spare pens on long trips, or for travel through genuinely hot climates where the bag itself might pass 86F.

The one hard rule: never freeze it

All four labels say the same thing: do not freeze the medication, and do not use it if it has been frozen. Freezing degrades the drug, and a thawed pen does not recover. This is the entire reason for the next two rules.

First, GLP-1 pens always fly in the carry-on, never in checked luggage. Cargo holds can freeze, and a frozen pen is a discarded pen. The cabin stays in a safe temperature range, so the carry-on is both the legal and the practical choice.

Second, if you do travel with a gel ice pack to keep the pen cool, never let the pen touch the ice directly. Wrap the pen in a cloth or keep it in its case inside the cooler bag. The goal is cool, not cold, and direct contact with a frozen pack is the easiest way to freeze a pen by accident.

At the airport

Declare the pen as injectable medication when you reach the checkpoint. TSA officers see injectable medications constantly. The pen goes through the X-ray in its case like any other medication, and you keep it with you, not in the overhead bin, during the flight.

TSA allows unused syringes and needles when they accompany injectable medication, and you do not need a prescription at the checkpoint, though keeping the pen in its original packaging with the pharmacy label makes the association obvious. Declare these items to the officer before screening begins.

For the ice pack: freeze gel packs solid before you leave for the airport. TSA requires ice packs to be frozen solid at screening, or they fall under the 3-1-1 liquids rule. The exception is the medical one: medically necessary gel ice packs in reasonable quantities are allowed even if they have started to melt or go slushy, but you must declare them as medical items. Either way, declaration is the move. Budget a few extra minutes, since the officer may swab the pack.

Keeping it cool on the road

Most trips need no cooling at all, per the room-temperature windows above. When you do need it, an insulated medication case with a gel ice pack covers flights, road trips, and hot days. A FRIO-style evaporative cooling wallet also works here: it keeps medication cool without any ice, which removes the freezing risk entirely. That is the same wallet on the insulin cooling guide, and the logic is identical.

At the hotel, the room fridge is fine, but treat it like any hotel fridge: keep the pen in its carton, away from the freezer compartment and the cooling element. Minibars run warm and are not for medication. If the room has no fridge and the trip is within your room-temperature window, the pen is fine in the bag, out of direct sun.

Injection day, time zones, and missed doses

Weekly dosing makes time zones nearly a non-issue. Take your dose on your usual weekday, wherever you are. If crossing zones shifts the calendar day, take it on the new day; the weekly rhythm matters more than the exact hour.

If you need to move your injection day by more than that, check the minimum gap on your label. Mounjaro's label allows changing the day as long as at least 3 days pass between doses. If the shift is large, or the travel schedule makes the day genuinely unclear, talk to your care team before the trip rather than improvising on the road.

Missed a dose mid-trip? Every label has a catch-up rule. For Mounjaro, take the missed dose as soon as you can if 4 days or fewer have passed since the scheduled day; if it has been longer, skip it and take the next dose on your regular day. For the other three, the label states the rule for your medication, and your pharmacist can confirm it in one call.

Sharps on the road

Used pens and needles do not go in the hotel trash. The labels direct used needles into an FDA-cleared sharps container right after use. For travel, a small portable sharps case, or a heavy-duty plastic container with a tight puncture-resistant lid, does the job until you get home. Single-dose pens (Wegovy, Mounjaro, Zepbound) are discarded whole after the injection; the Ozempic multi-dose pen stays in use for its 56 days with a fresh needle each time.

International trips

Keep the pen in its original packaging with the pharmacy label showing your name, and carry a copy of the prescription, digital or printed. These medications are prescription-only in most countries, and the labeled packaging plus a prescription copy answers every question a border officer or pharmacist is likely to ask. Check your destination's rules for injectable medications before you fly, especially for longer stays: availability and brand names vary by country, so do not plan on replacing a lost pen abroad without research.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do GLP-1 pens need to stay refrigerated while traveling?

Usually not for a normal trip. An in-use Ozempic pen keeps 56 days at room temperature up to 86F. A Wegovy pen keeps 28 days at room temperature before the cap comes off. Mounjaro and Zepbound single-dose pens keep 21 days at room temperature. Unopened spare pens should stay refrigerated when you can manage it, and no GLP-1 pen should ever freeze.

Can I put my Ozempic or Wegovy pen in checked luggage?

No. Cargo holds can freeze, and every one of these labels says the same thing: do not freeze, and do not use the pen if it has been frozen. Keep GLP-1 pens in your carry-on, where the temperature stays in the cabin range.

What do I tell TSA about my GLP-1 pen?

Declare it as injectable medication at the checkpoint. Pens go through the X-ray in their case like any medication. TSA allows unused syringes and needles when they accompany injectable medication. If you carry a gel ice pack to keep the pen cool, freeze it solid before security; medically necessary gel packs are allowed even if they have started to melt, but you must declare them.

What if I miss my weekly dose while traveling?

Check your medication's label for the catch-up rule. For Mounjaro, take the missed dose as soon as you can if it has been 4 days or less, and skip it if it has been longer, returning to your regular day. Mounjaro's label also allows changing your injection day as long as at least 3 days pass between doses. For the others, the label states the minimum gap, and your care team can confirm the plan before you leave.
