Dexcom G7 in Heat and Cold: Temperature Limits for Travel (2026)

Dexcom publishes exact temperature numbers for the G7, and they are narrower than most people assume. Here are the specs, what heat and cold actually do to your sensor, and how to pack spares for extreme climates.

Updated September 2026  ·  6 min read

A parked car in summer hits 130°F inside. A parked car on a winter night drops below freezing. Both of those will ruin spare Dexcom G7 sensors, and most travelers have no idea until a sensor fails mid-trip for no apparent reason.

I am still on the G6 myself, but the temperature physics is the same for both generations. Every number below comes straight from Dexcom's G7 user guide, linked at the bottom.

The Official Numbers

Dexcom lists separate ranges for operating (the sensor on your body, working) and storage (spare sensors in their packaging). They are not the same, and the storage range is the one travelers violate most.

Component Operating range Storage range
G7 sensor (worn) 50°F to 107.6°F (10°C to 42°C) 36°F to 86°F (2°C to 30°C), cool dry place, keep in sterile packaging
G7 receiver 32°F to 104°F (0°C to 40°C) 32°F to 104°F (0°C to 40°C), never in a freezer

Stored correctly, Dexcom rates the sensor shelf life at up to 18 months. Stored badly, a sensor can be dead before you ever open it.

What Heat Actually Does

Heat attacks on two fronts. The first is your spare sensors. Heat degrades the sensor chemistry inside the sealed package, and a car interior in summer blows past the 86°F storage ceiling within an hour. A sensor that baked in a glovebox for an afternoon may look fine and fail on day two. You will never connect the two events.

The second front is the sensor you are wearing. Here the news is better. Your body keeps a worn sensor near body temperature regardless of the weather, so ambient heat barely registers for it. The real heat problem on your body is adhesion. Sweat loosens the adhesive faster than anything else, which is why sensors peel off on beach trips and hiking days. Overpatches and skin-prep wipes solve most of it. I cover the full adhesion routine in my Dexcom G7 airport security guide.

Never leave spare sensors in a parked car. Not in summer, not "just for an hour," not in the trunk where you assume it stays cooler. It does not. Summer interiors destroy the 86°F storage limit fast, and winter nights drop below the 36°F floor. Your carry-on stays with you. The car does not.

What Cold Actually Does

Cold is the mirror image. A worn sensor sits against warm skin under your clothes, so normal winter days and even ski trips do not push it out of range. Body heat does the work for you.

Spare sensors are the concern again. Do not leave them in a freezing car overnight or in an unheated bag on a cold tarmac. The storage floor is 36°F, and an hour in a freezing trunk gets you there.

Then there is the weak link nobody thinks about: your phone. Phones give up in extreme cold long before the sensor does. Screen goes dim, battery percentage collapses, and suddenly your display device is a brick in your ski jacket. Keep the phone in an inside pocket, close to your body, on very cold days. If you own the Dexcom receiver, it tolerates cold better than most phones and makes a solid backup.

Packing for Hot Climates

  • All spare sensors ride in your carry-on. Always. Cargo holds freeze and tarmacs bake, so checked luggage is out.
  • A small insulated pouch keeps supplies at a stable temperature through long travel days. Sensors do not need refrigeration.
  • Dexcom allows fridge storage (36°F to 86°F) but never the freezer. Room temperature is simpler and avoids condensation, so that is what I would do.
  • Pack overpatches. Heat plus sweat is the number one sensor killer on tropical trips, and a patch costs almost nothing.

Packing for Cold Climates

  • Spare sensors stay insulated in your carry-on, inside your jacket or bag rather than an outer pocket.
  • If a spare has been sitting in the cold, let it warm up indoors before you put it on.
  • Phone in an inside pocket on bitter days. Bring a portable charger, since cold drains batteries faster.
  • Keep your backup meter with you rather than in a cold bag, and keep fast-acting glucose somewhere it will not freeze solid.

If Your Readings Act Strange in Extreme Temperatures

Outside its operating range, the sensor may throw errors, drop signal, or give readings you cannot trust. The rule does not change with the weather: if the numbers do not match how you feel, do not make treatment decisions off them. Use your fingerstick meter until the sensor settles or you replace it. That is Dexcom's standing guidance for any suspicious reading, and extreme temperatures are exactly the kind of situation it exists for.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the Dexcom G7 storage temperature range?

Spare sensors must be stored between 36°F and 86°F (2°C to 30°C), in a cool dry place, kept in their sterile packaging until use. Dexcom rates shelf life at up to 18 months when stored correctly.

Can I leave Dexcom G7 sensors in a hot car?

No. A parked car in summer exceeds the 86°F storage limit within an hour, and heat degrades the sensor chemistry inside the sealed package. Keep spare sensors in your carry-on with you, never in a parked car.

Does heat affect Dexcom G7 accuracy?

A worn sensor is kept near body temperature, so ambient heat affects it less than you would expect. Extreme heat mainly threatens spare sensors in storage and sensor adhesion, since sweat loosens the adhesive. If readings do not match your symptoms, check with a fingerstick meter.

Can I wear my Dexcom G7 skiing or in freezing weather?

Yes. Body heat keeps a worn sensor within its operating range under normal winter clothing. The more common cold-weather failure is your phone, which shuts down in extreme cold long before the sensor struggles. Keep your phone in an inside pocket.

Can spare Dexcom G7 sensors be refrigerated?

Dexcom allows refrigerator storage between 36°F and 86°F, but never the freezer. Room temperature storage is simpler and avoids condensation, so most travelers just keep spares at room temperature.

What is the Dexcom G7 receiver temperature range?

The receiver operates and stores between 32°F and 104°F (0°C to 40°C). Do not store it in a freezer.
